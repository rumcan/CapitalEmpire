# P21: The fairway tool, rethought (Course > Build)

Status: **proposal** (research only, no code), 2026-10-24. Written from the `theme-consistency` worktree at `c8cf2ee`.

The owner, looking at Course > Build > Fairway: "wtf is this Build fairway tool. Who ever built this must be joking. It's terrible.
Needs a research and rethink." And: "the edges of painted texture needs a light feather and a bevel, with shadow and light."

The owner's direction for the redesign (added mid-research, and the core of this plan):
1. "The golf course should sit on a tiny isometric grid, and each placed texture on the grid fills that space but joins neighbours
   with the same texture like water droplets."
2. "The entire thing should be very animated, with CSS and rounded shapes."
3. "Maybe a little ground splash animation when you click and place the texture, and maybe it starts as dirt and then the grass
   fades in as time moves on."

## 0. The plan on one screen

- **One data model: the course grid.** The course is a grid of 1 m cells (half a tile). Each cell holds one surface (rough, fescue,
  water, fairway, tee, bunker, waste, green), its hole and the month it was planted. Polygons stop being the source of truth for
  the course; they are drawn *from* the grid.
- **Droplet outlines.** The grid is never drawn as squares. A metaball field over the filled cells is contoured with marching
  squares. A lone cell becomes a round dot, neighbours flow together, inner corners get fillets, and cells that touch only at a
  corner join with a neck. That is "like water droplets".
- **Two ways to fill cells.**
  - **New hole:** click the tee, click the green, drag the centre line. The fairway grows along it with real widths: narrow at
    the start, widest at the landing zone, pinched before the green. Rough and first cut follow by themselves. Par, length and
    price show live.
  - **Cell brushes:** Fairway, Green, Tee, Bunker, Water, Rough, Fescue. Every dab merges with its neighbours. Stamps (kidney green,
    pot bunker, pond) are one-click brushes.
- **The look.** Soft feathered edges. A gentle bevel lit from the upper right: a light rim on the sun side, a soft shade on the
  lower left, and inverted for bunkers and water. Automatic collars (first cut, fringe, bunker lip, bank) as textures, never
  outlines. Mowing stripes that follow the hole's line. No orange rim, no dark outline strokes.
- **Alive.** The hover cell breathes. A placed dab splashes dirt and the new blob squashes into place. Seeded ground starts as dirt
  and grass comes in as patches over its grow-in months. The panel is rounded pills that spring when pressed.
- **Instant re-bake.** The grid is cut into 64 x 64-tile course chunks. An edit re-contours only the chunks it touches and sends one
  lot-ground patch per chunk. The ground re-bakes itself. There is never a "rebuild ground" step.

## 1. What is there today (read in the code)

### 1.1 How a player builds a hole today, step by step

1. Open Course (C). In a golf game the tool opens on **Pieces** with **Fairway** in hand (`paint-tool.ts:262`, `paint-tool.ts:185`).
2. Pick a hole in the hole strip (`HoleStrip.tsx`). The panel offers Build / Presets / Plants, then four methods: Pieces, Brush,
   Shape, Height (`CoursePanel.tsx:35-51`).
3. **Pieces:** click to stamp the piece. The "Fairway" piece is a rectangle 24 x 12 tiles (48 x 24 m) (`course-snap/index.ts:95`,
   `:223-225`). It snaps end-on to the fairway it touches and turns to face away from it (`:302-342`). R turns and [ ] sizes, but
   only in Advanced (`progressive-ui/index.ts:160`, `CoursePanel.tsx:71-75`). Each click lays four stacked areas.
4. **Brush:** drag a round dab (fairway radius 5 tiles). It fills a 0.5-tile cell mask, and on release the mask is traced, smoothed
   and simplified into polygons that replace the hole's areas of that kind (`brush.ts:7`, `paint-tool.ts:654-686`).
5. **Shape:** click corners for a straight-edged polygon in the surface's style (`paint-tool.ts:254-255`).
6. **Presets:** place a generated hole (`holepreset.ts:28`) or one of the studio's 18 slots. All 18 slots in
   `hole-presets.json` are empty today.
7. After every change the engine fits the pin to the green and the three tee markers to the tee (`holeview.ts:66-89`). Par and length
   come from the tee-to-pin line (`holes.ts:124-150`).
8. A dogleg cannot be told to the game: aim points cannot be placed (`COURSE-BUILDER.md:78-79`). A bent fairway can warn "Most of the
   line is off the course" (`holes.ts:145`).

### 1.2 Why it looks bad

1. **The default fairway is a rectangle.** `fairway-corridor` falls through to a four-corner box (`course-snap/index.ts:223-225`) with a
   3-tile corner radius (`:127`). Chained by snapping, the boxes make the owner's "big rounded rectangles in an L".
2. **The hard orange-brown rim is a real layer.** A fairway piece is four stacked areas (`course-snap/index.ts:252-254`), each with a
   2 m border band (`BORDER_W[3] = 1` tile, `paint.ts:96`):
   - shadow-bevel: rough, border **pine straw** (orange-brown), multiply tone;
   - outer-rim: rough, first-cut border;
   - collar: first cut, fringe border;
   - core: fairway, first-cut border.
   The shadow layer is shifted 0.55 tiles down-left, so its pine-straw band shows as a rim, thickest at the lower left.
3. **The "bevels" are not offsets.** `offsetPolygonRadial` (`course-snap/index.ts:167-179`) pushes each corner away from the centroid.
   On a long shape the band is uneven (wide at the ends, thin along the sides). On a concave shape (dogleg, kidney) it can cross itself.
4. **Every edge is a hard cut with a dark line.** Each bordered area strokes `rgba(0,0,0,0.28)` under its band (`renderer.ts:622`).
   Four layers give four hard lines per edge. Nothing is feathered.
5. **The "fake tile pattern" is the texture.** The fairway preset mixes two photos in patches (`paint-presets.json`, "fairway":
   the fringe photo and the fairway photo at x4.4, patch 0.25). The fairway photo (`src/assets/ground/preset-fairway.webp`) has visible
   square block artefacts. Pieces use the raw `golfFairway` photo (`course-snap/index.ts:127`); brushes use the preset (`paint.ts:287`).
   Side by side, the two methods do not match.
6. **Stripes run the wrong way.** The preset's mowing stripes are baked in tile space (`paint-presets.ts:30`; fairway: on, along y,
   2 tiles). They follow a map axis, not the hole. Pieces turn the texture with the piece, so stripes run *across* each piece and
   change direction at every join.
7. **The starting course is boxy.** The old nine's fairways are bands with square corners (`coursegen.ts:133-158`), half width 7.5
   tiles (`:165`), rounded only by a 4-tile radius (`paintcourse.ts:9`), along a rectangle's sides. That is the first thing a new golf
   game shows.

### 1.3 Why it feels bad

8. **The price is three times too high.** The ghost said "Fairway · hole 1 · $6,943". I rebuilt that piece with the real modules
   (`E:\ClaudeTemp\fairway-research\check.ts`): core fairway 1,152 m² $2,304, collar $2,028, outer rim $1,277, shadow $1,334. `pieceCost`
   charges every hidden overlapping layer (`course-pieces/index.ts:53-57`). The fair price of what you see is about $2,900.
9. **No hole in the tool.** Pieces and brushes do not know the hole's line, par or landing zone. The player stamps shapes and hopes.
10. **Too many methods, none good.** Pieces, Brush, Shape, Height and Presets each give a different look and different rules.
11. **A new player cannot even turn the piece.** Turn, size and snap are Advanced only, so the first fairway is a fixed 48 x 24 m box.
12. **The ghost is noise.** It draws four stacked outlines (`paint-tool.ts:456`) and a label with no area, no par and no length.
13. **Seeded ground pops.** An immature area swaps its whole texture for `golfSod` until ready (`paint-tool.ts:204`). No fade.

### 1.4 What is worth keeping

- The brush's **cell mask** (`brush.ts`): the owner's grid, half-built. Its weakness is the lossy round trip: masks are rebuilt from
  polygons on each stroke (`paint-tool.ts:656`, and the comment at `:668`).
- The generated hole's fairway is **a centre line plus a width** (`holepreset.ts:12-13`, `:35-38`). That is the right model.
- The piece **archetype shapes** (kidney, peanut, practice green; crescent and pot bunker; pond; creek) (`course-snap/index.ts:190-226`):
  good stamps.
- `holes.ts` and `holeview.ts` (par, length, warnings, automatic pin and tee markers), `costs.ts` (prices, `growInShare` at `:81`),
  the lot-ground channel with per-key re-bakes (`ground-cache.ts:67-77`), the ghost style (`ext/ghost-preview`), and the unlock
  stages (`ext/progressive-ui`).

**Sid Meier's SimGolf (2002).**

### 2.3 Real course architecture (numbers for our defaults)

- **Fairway width.** US Open setups are tight: Sahalee about 24 m, a 27 yd fairway is "tight" for Gil Hanse
  ([Links: narrowest holes](https://linksmagazine.com/10-of-golfs-narrowest-holes/)). Shinnecock averaged 41 m in 2026, widest 57 m
  ([Golf Today](https://golftoday.co.uk/shinnecock-hills-2026-us-open-fairways/)). Kapalua averages 41–46 m
  ([Links: widest](https://linksmagazine.com/top-10-widest-american-courses-you-can-play/)). Longer holes want wider fairways; an
  open green front allows a narrower fairway near the green ([ASGCA, Brauer](https://asgca.org/jeffrey-brauer-asgca-comments-on-widening-fairways/)).
  So: **25–55 m, member courses 32–46 m.**
- **Landing zones.** Scratch drive 229 m, bogey drive 183 m ([Golf.com, USGA rating](https://golf.com/travel/how-golf-hole-rated-explained-usga-expert/)).
  Landing areas are wider and flatter. A near landing pairs with a wide, shallow green; a far one with a narrow, deep green
  ([Ed Le Beau](https://edlebeau.substack.com/p/the-wrong-tee-box)). So: **landing zone at about 210 m.**
- **Greens.** PGA Tour average 530 m², Pebble Beach 325 m², TPC San Antonio 595 m²
  ([TGL](https://tglgolf.com/articles/2025/03/greenzone/tgl-green-size--how-it-compares); [GolfPass](https://www.golfpass.com/travel-advisor/articles/pebble-beach-us-open-small-greens)).
  So: **400–700 m², default 550 m².**
- **Collars and first cut.** "No set width for a collar"; make it one mower width ([Golfdom](https://www.golfdom.com/?p=37534)). Greens
  mowers are 0.5–1.5 m wide ([Club and Resort Business](https://clubandresortbusiness.com/what-are-the-two-basic-types-of-greens-mowers/)).
  First cut about 1.8 m *(unverified)*. Heights of cut: greens 1/8 in, collars 1/4 in, fairways 3/8 in, second cut 1 3/8 in
  ([Golf.com](https://golf.com/travel/difference-masters-us-open-rough/)). So: **fringe 1 m, first cut 2 m.**
- **Tees.** 100–200 sq ft per 1,000 rounds a year ([GCM](https://www.gcmonline.com/course/profiles/news/tee-box-restoration-at-medford-village-country-club)).
  Par by length and several tees per hole ([Wikipedia: Golf course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_course)).
- **Shape language.** Fairway lines should be "simple and flowing" and follow the land; tight wiggles are "squiggles"
  ([Links: Hanse](https://linksmagazine.com/gil-hanse-explains-the-importance-of-fairways/)). Capes and bunkers at the corners of
  doglegs ([Top 100: template holes](https://www.top100golfcourses.com/news-item/r-is-for-road-template-holes)). So: **edges wave
  over 40–70 m, never in short wiggles.**
- **Mowing.** Half-and-half (one side mown toward the tee, one toward the green), checkerboard, contour stripes, push-pull
  ([Club and Resort Business: stripes](https://clubandresortbusiness.com/earning-stripes/)). Augusta mows toward the tee
  ([Golf.com](https://golf.com/lifestyle/golf-courses-choose-mowing-patterns/)). Diagonals cost extra turns
  ([GCM](https://www.gcmonline.com/news/the-road-less-traveled)). Greens are striped less often
  ([Golf Compendium](https://www.golfcompendium.com/2020/02/striping-stripes-in-golf-grass.html)). So: **stripes follow the hole
  line by default; greens unstriped.**

### 2.4 Merging cells into droplets

- **Metaballs and marching squares** ([Jamie Wong](https://jamie-wong.com/2014/08/19/metaballs-and-marching-squares/),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marching_squares), [Paul Bourke](http://paulbourke.net/geometry/polygonise/)).
  - The field sums each ball's contribution; inside is field >= threshold. Summing (not taking the max) is what makes neighbours
    bulge toward each other and fill inner corners.
  - Marching squares samples only the lattice corners: a 4-bit case per square, 16 cases, and the crossing on each edge at
    t = (T − fa) / (fb − fa). Cases 5 and 10 are saddles: decide by the average of the four corners.
  - Per-pixel sampling is far too slow; corner sampling is cheap and local, so only the squares near an edit need redoing.
- **Smoothing and rounded unions.** Chaikin cuts each segment at 1/4 and 3/4 ([BIT-101](https://bit-101.com/2017/2021/08/chaikins-algorithm-drawing-curves/)).
  Quilez's smooth minimum `h = clamp(0.5 + 0.5(b−a)/k, 0, 1); d = mix(b, a, h) − k·h·(1−h)` gives concave fillets of width k
  ([glsl-smooth-min](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/glslify/glsl-smooth-min/master/poly.glsl)). The CSS "gooey" filter (blur, then
  an alpha threshold) is the image-space version, too costly on large areas ([CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/gooey-effect/)).
- **Townscaper** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/blogs/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making),
  [Sylves tutorial](https://boristhebrave.com/docs/sylves/1/articles/tutorials/townscaper.html),
  [Retroware](https://articles.retroware.com/2021/12/24/townscaper-and-the-art-of-minimalism/)).
  - Each placement re-solves its neighbours; pieces are corner modules (marching-cubes style).
  - The structure appears first and decoration fills in a moment later.
  - It "is allowed to fail silently": the player never sees an error.
  - Rules are learnable (gardens only form in enclosed spaces). "I want the user to feel like they're in control of the large shapes."
  - Plunk sounds, no goal, removing costs nothing.
- **The dual grid** ([Excalibur](https://excaliburjs.com/blog/Dual%20Tilemap%20Autotiling%20Technique),
  [Red Blob: grid parts](https://www.redblobgames.com/grids/parts/)). Draw a display grid offset by half a cell; each display tile reads
  the four cells at its corners, so 5 rounded shapes cover every case. A real option for tile art. We do not take it for the ground
  (section 3.3) because our surfaces are ground-space photos that must feather and take sun-lit bevels per edge direction.

## 3. The proposal

### 3.1 The course grid

- **Cell size: 1 m (0.5 tile).** "Tiny", as the owner asked. It matches the brush mask already in the game (`brush.ts:7`). A fringe is
  one cell and a first cut two. A green is about 550 cells, a par-4 fairway about 10,000.
  - The 2 m alternative (one tile) halves the work but its staircases scallop about 1 m on shallow diagonals (measured, section 3.2).
- **One surface per cell**, in draw order from low to high: none (map grass) 0, rough 1, fescue 2, water 3, fairway 4, tee 5,
  bunker 6, waste 7, green 8. Cart paths leave the course painter: they become P19's path network (section 3.9).
- **Per cell also:** the hole (0–18) and the planted month (for grow-in).
- **Extent:** the bounding box of the course's owned land plus a margin. It grows (re-allocated) when land is bought. A 1 x 1 km course
  is 1 M cells, 4 MB in memory (1 + 1 + 2 bytes).
- **Saved** as run-length varints in base64: a few tens of kB for a full course.

### 3.2 Droplet outlines (the maths)

The grid becomes outlines in four steps, per surface:
1. **Occupancy** of the surface's cells. Optionally a **closing** (dilate then erode by r cells) for bigger fillets. Default r: fairway
   2, water 2, rough 2, green 1, bunker 1, tee 0 (tees stay crisp).
2. **Metaball field** on a 0.25-tile (0.5 m) lattice: each filled cell centre adds `k(d) = (1 − d²/R²)³` for d < R, with
   **R = 1.25 cells**.
3. **Marching squares** at **T = 0.62**, with linear interpolation on each edge and the saddle rule.
4. **Relax** (optional, 0–2 Taubin passes, λ 0.5, μ −0.53) to calm staircase waves, then simplify at 0.04 tiles.

I measured R and T numerically (`E:\ClaudeTemp\fairway-research\kern.js`, `wob.js`), in cell units:

| Case | Result with R = 1.25, T = 0.62 |
| --- | --- |
| A lone cell | a round dot of radius 0.48 cell (it nearly fills its cell) |
| A straight run of cells | the edge stays on the cell boundary (wobble 0.015 cell) |
| A one-cell-wide line | half width 0.49 cell |
| Two cells touching at a corner | the field there is 1.02 x T: they join with a thin neck |
| An inner L corner | a fillet of radius about 0.55 cell (more with closing) |
| Two cells one empty cell apart | stay apart (0.15 x T in the gap) |
| A 1:1 / 1:2 / 1:3 staircase | waves of 0.25 / 0.52 / 0.65 cell before relax |

At 1 m cells those waves are 0.25–0.65 m: a soft, hand-mown edge. The relax pass brings them under 0.15 m where the surface wants
calm (fairway, water). Larger rounding comes from closing, not from a bigger R, because a bigger R shrinks lone cells
(R = 1.5 gives a 0.26-cell dot; R = 2 makes a lone cell vanish).

**Why vector outlines and not dual-grid tile art:** our surfaces are seamless photos laid in ground space. Outlines let the renderer
fill them, feather them, and light each edge by its own direction. Tile art would need a corner set per surface pair and could not
take the sun.

**Chunks.** The grid is processed in 64 x 64-tile chunks (128 x 128 cells). Each chunk gives, per surface:
- **fill rings**: closed rings clipped to the chunk box grown by 0.1 tile. Neighbouring fills overlap by 0.1 tile; the texture is in
  ground space, so the overlap is invisible and there are no hairline seams;
- **edge polylines**: open polylines on the true boundary only, cut exactly at the chunk box, never along it. These carry the feather,
  the bevel and the collar;
- **collar rings** (section 3.5).
The field near a seam is computed from the global grid (the chunk plus a 3-tile margin), so both sides see the same values and the
edges meet exactly. Relax runs on the margin-extended contour before the cut.

### 3.3 The interaction model

**A. New hole (the main way).**
1. **Click "New hole".** A tee ghost (a rounded tee box) follows the cursor. Near a cart path end or the last green it snaps there
   (P19), and the label reads "Hole 4 · tee · 85 m walk from hole 3".
2. **Click the green.** While you move, a live hole ghost runs from the tee to the cursor: centre line, fairway, green, rough
   corridor, as droplet outlines. The label shows "Hole 4 · par 4 · 382 m · $34,900". Par snaps visibly when the length crosses
   230 m or 430 m (`holes.ts:59`).
3. **Shape it.** The plan stays open with handles:
   - drag the centre line anywhere to add a bend (up to 3; they become the hole's aim points, which fixes the dogleg warning);
   - drag a round width handle on either edge to widen or narrow the fairway there;
   - drag the tee or the green; R turns the green; [ ] changes its size (400–700 m²).
   Every drag re-plans the ghost at once.
4. **Build** (click the gold pill or press Enter). The cells fill as a ripple from tee to green, starting as dirt, and the hole strip
   chip pops. One undo step.
5. **Edit later.** Click a hole's line in Hole focus to get its handles back. A plan edit changes only the cells that differ between
   the old and new footprint, and never cells you painted yourself (bunkers, water, other holes).

The plan's defaults (from section 2.3), in metres along the line from the back tee:
- **Par 3:** no fairway; an approach apron 18 m wide over the last 25 m before the green.
- **Par 4:** fairway from 100 m. Widths: 30 at 100, **40 at the landing zone (210)**, 28 at 50 m before the green, 22 at the green front.
- **Par 5:** as par 4, plus 32 at 300 and a second landing of 38 at 390.
- **Edges** wave with a seeded low swell: wavelength 40–70 m, at most 8% of the width (2.5 m), zero at the ends. On a dogleg the
  outside of the bend gets 15% more width.
- **Green** 550 m², shape round / kidney / peanut / pear (the archetypes). It is deeper when the approach is long, wider when short.
- **Tee** a rounded strip 28 x 10 m along the line (back, middle and forward markers fit themselves: `holeview.ts:50`).
- **Rough** fills empty cells within 30 m of the line (the 60 m corridor, GOLF-PLAN 4.2).

**B. Cell brushes (touch-ups and free design).**
- Surfaces: Fairway, Green, Tee, Bunker, Water, Rough, Fescue (and Waste in Advanced).
- Sizes: 1, 3, 5, 9, 15 m ([ ] keys). The hover shows the exact footprint as droplets.
- **Fill around by default:** a brush changes only cells below it in the order (a fairway stroke slides past a green without
  eating it). Shift paints over anything. Skipped cells are not an error; the ghost simply does not include them.
- **Rub out** (Alt or the pill) turns playing surfaces back to rough; on rough it clears to map grass. Erasing refunds nothing
  (GOLF-PLAN 4.1).
- **Stamps:** kidney, peanut and practice greens; crescent and pot bunkers; pond; creek. One click, R turns, [ ] sizes. They are the
  archetype polygons rasterised into cells, so they merge like everything else.
- The brush paints for the current hole. A stroke is one undo step.

**C. Advanced** (the existing "Show all tools" switch): lasso fill (press and drag an outline, it fills with cells), roundness and
relax per surface, collar widths, stripe pattern and width, seed or sod, exact width numbers on the plan handles, earthworks shaping
(raised green and tee, sunken bunker: the piece height ops, kept), and the cell grid always visible.

### 3.4 The look

**Textures.** One fairway texture everywhere: a clean, unstriped fairway grass photo (an art ticket: today's photo has block
artefacts). Stripes are drawn by the renderer along the hole (below), not baked into the photo.

**Collars are automatic and are textures, not lines.**

| Surface | Collar | Width | Laid over |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fairway | first cut | 2 m | none, rough, fescue |
| Green | fringe | 1 m | anything but water and bunker |
| Tee | first cut | 1 m | none, rough, fescue |
| Bunker | grass lip (rough) | 1 m | anything but water |
| Water | bank (rocky dirt) | 1 m | none, rough, fescue |

A collar ring is the same field run on the occupancy dilated by the collar width, so it is round and even. Collars are priced
(first cut $1.5/m², fringe $4/m²).

**Edges: a light feather and a bevel, with shadow and light** (the owner's rule: the sun at the upper right, cast shadows at 145°).
For each edge segment, take its outward normal n in tile space and the sun factor `L = n · SUN_TILE_DIR` (−1..1;
`ext/course-height/index.ts:22`).
- **Raised surfaces** (green, tee, fairway slightly): a light rim inside the edge where L > 0 (the upper-right edges), a soft shade
  inside where L < 0 (the lower-left edges), and a soft drop shadow just outside the lower-left edges.
- **Sunken surfaces** (bunker, water): inverted. The lip shades the inside of the upper-right edge; the lower-left inner face catches
  the light.
- Every strength is scaled by |L|, so edges side-on to the sun get nothing.
- **No outline strokes.** The 0.28 black stroke (`renderer.ts:622`) and the stacked rim layers go for course surfaces.

| Surface | Feather (out) | Light rim (in, L > 0) | Shade (in, L < 0) | Drop shadow (out, L < 0) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Green | 0.3 m | 0.8 m, screen `#fff4c8`, 0.22 | 0.8 m, multiply `#233018`, 0.22 | offset 0.4 m, soft 0.8 m, 0.16 |
| Tee | 0.3 m | 1.0 m, 0.26 | 1.0 m, 0.26 | offset 0.6 m, soft 1.0 m, 0.20 |
| Fairway | 0.4 m | 1.2 m, 0.12 | 1.2 m, 0.12 | none |
| First cut, fringe | 0.4 m | none | none | none |
| Rough | 1.0 m (melts into the map grass) | none | 0.6 m, 0.06 | none |
| Fescue | 1.2 m, broken by noise | none | none | none |
| Bunker (sunken) | 0.15 m (a crisp lip) | lower-left inside 0.6 m, screen `#fff2d6`, 0.20 | upper-right inside 0.9 m, multiply `#3a2a18`, 0.30 | none |
| Water (sunken) | 0.2 m | lower-left inside glint 0.25 m, 0.25 | upper-right inside 1.4 m, multiply `#0d1a2a`, 0.35 | none |

How to draw it cheaply (a note for the engineer fixing edges now):
- **Feather outward:** stroke the edge polylines with the surface's own pattern at three widths (2f, 1.33f, 0.67f) and alpha 1/3 each,
  unclipped, before the fill. That makes a linear fade without a scratch canvas.
- **Rims and shade:** bin edge segments by L into 8 bins, with normals smoothed over a 2 m window so staircase waves do not flicker.
  Stroke each bin's segments as one path, clipped inside, round caps, three nested strokes for the fade.
- **Drop shadow:** fill the outline offset toward the shadow direction, multiply, three nested feather strokes, under the fill.
- Shade colours should come from the cast-shadow colour so all shade in the game matches. Keep strengths low: the T028 hillshade
  lights real height on top.
- The same recipe should serve the Paint tool's decor areas, so one shared function fits both.

**Mowing stripes follow the hole.** The fairway patch carries the hole's centre line. The renderer lays stripe bands along it:
- **Along** (default): bands parallel to the centre line, 3 m wide (a fairway mower's pass), alternating light (screen `#b4d46c`,
  0.10) and dark (multiply `#2e5a1c`, 0.10), the owner's preset colours.
- **Across**, **checkerboard**, **half-and-half** (one side of the centre line light, the other dark) and **none** in Advanced.
- Bands turn with doglegs. Greens are unstriped by default. Fairway cells with no plan use the hole's tee-to-pin line.

**Grow-in: dirt first, then grass comes in.**
- A seeded cell starts as **dirt** (the soil photo). Its grass share is `growInShare(surface, 'seed', months)` (`costs.ts:81`), stepped
  to quarters (0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1).
- The bake draws the grown surface, then dirt over it through a **growth mask**: a tiny per-chunk image (1 px per cell) with alpha =
  1 − share, multiplied by the existing patch noise (`renderer.ts:530`, `blendMask`). Grass therefore arrives as **patches that
  spread**, not a uniform fade.
- Sod shows the sod-roll look (`golfSod`) for one month, then the surface.
- The engine already re-sends growing ground each month (`engine.ts:2928`). With quarter steps a chunk re-bakes at most four times
  during its grow-in.

### 3.5 The ghost, the price and the animation

**The ghost shows what will happen before it happens** (the UIX rule 1).
- **Hover:** the brush footprint as droplets (the same outline maths on a scratch window), filled in the surface colour at 30%.
- **During a stroke:** the merged result of the stroke so far, with its collar.
- **Wizard:** the full hole: line, fairway, green, tee, rough corridor, collars, and the stripes faintly.
- **Red** cells where it cannot go (outside owned land, under a road or building), with the reason beside the cursor.
- **Labels** (one line, `ghostStatus` style):
  - brush: "Fairway · +46 m² · $92";
  - rub out: "Rough · 46 m² · free";
  - wizard: "Hole 4 · par 4 · 382 m · $34,900";
  - red: "Outside your land" or "Not enough money: $34,900 (you have $20,000)".
- **Price = only the cells that change**, by surface (seed or sod), plus new collar area. Never the hidden layers.
- The hole card shows the split: fairway, green, tee, rough, collars.

**Map animation** (canvas; the engine redraws the overlay every frame, `engine.ts:3468`, so `PaintView` gains `now`):
- **Hover breathing:** outline alpha 0.6 to 0.9 and scale 1.00 to 1.03 about the cursor, a 1.4 s sine.
- **Grid hint:** while a brush is active, cell diamonds fade in within 8 m of the cursor and fade out with distance. No full-map grid.
- **Splash on place:** at each committed dab and on release, 8–12 particles (dirt brown; sand pale; water blue droplets) thrown up
  and out, with gravity in screen y, 450 ms life. Plus an iso ring ripple growing from 0.5x to 1.6x the brush radius in 300 ms.
- **Squash:** the new blob, drawn in its colour over the map, scales 0.90, then 1.04, then 1.00 about the click over 260 ms
  (ease-out-back). It fades out when the ground bake of its chunks lands, or after 400 ms. This also hides the worker's bake delay.
- **Wizard build:** the line draws itself in 300 ms, then cells pop along the line in arc-length order over 600 ms.
- **No screen shake** except a trauma of 0.1 when a whole hole is built.
- **Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`) or the gear menu's "Effects off": fades only, no particles, no scaling.
- Sound hooks for later: a soft plunk, pitch varied ±10%.

**Panel motion (CSS, rounded):**
- Pills everywhere (`border-radius: 9999px`). Surface chips are round droplet swatches of the real photo.
- Press spring: `transform: scale(.94)` then back with a `linear()` spring easing in 220 ms.
- The chosen chip glows once. A hole strip chip pops (scale 1, 1.12, 1 in 300 ms) when its hole becomes valid.
- Tab content slides and fades in 180 ms.
- Only `transform` and `opacity` animate. A `prefers-reduced-motion` block turns transforms into fades.
- Today `index.css` has no keyframes and no reduced-motion rule.

### 3.8 Performance and the instant re-bake

- **Patches:** one lot-ground key per course chunk, `cg:<cx>:<cy>`, holding that chunk's surface layers in draw order. The ground
  cache invalidates only that chunk's box and keeps the old bake until the new one lands (`ground-cache.ts:67-77`, `:86`). The
  ground re-bakes itself; there is never a manual step.
- **Main-thread work per edit:** a dab touches 1–4 chunks. Per chunk and changed surface: splat about 16 k cells into a 257 x 257
  lattice (plus margin) and march it. That is roughly 1–3 ms in JS. A dab changes at most two surfaces.
  - During a drag the ghost works on the dab's window only. Chunk patches go out at most every 120 ms (the existing `SEND_MS`) and on
    release.
  - **Budget: at most 4 ms main-thread time per dab**, checked by a new bench case.
- **Ground work:** a chunk patch has at most 8 surface layers. District (lite) chunks draw fill and collars only: no stripes,
  feathers or rims.
- **Memory:** 4 MB per km² of grid; contours are cached per chunk and rebuilt only when the chunk's revision changes.
- **Limits:** chunk patches have their own point cap (4,000 per surface per chunk, simplified at 0.04 tile), not `MAX_PTS`.

### 3.9 Fit with P19 (course drives and cart paths)

- Cart paths leave the course grid: they are P19's path network (T068, T070), drawn with the road tool.
- Cells under roads, drives and paths are skipped and not charged (roads draw over paint anyway).
- The wizard's tee snaps within 6 tiles of a cart path end or the previous green's path, and warns over the 150 m walk (`holes.ts:21`).
- After Build, a dashed **suggested cart path** (last green to this tee, then along the rough to the green) can be built with one
  click once T070 lands.
- A road or drive crossing the hole's corridor shows red with "A road crosses the hole: add a cart crossing" (GOLF-PLAN 4.2).

### 3.10 Decisions this plan recommends

1. **1 m cells** (not 2 m): tiny, smooth, and matches `brush.ts`.
2. **Vector droplets** from a metaball field (R 1.25 cells, T 0.62), not tile art.
3. **Collars are automatic textures**, priced; first cut cells can be painted by hand only in Advanced.
4. **Fill around** is the default brush rule; Shift overwrites.
5. **Plans re-rasterise by footprint diff**, so hand touch-ups survive plan edits.
6. **Pieces retire** for course surfaces; their shapes live on as stamps.
7. **Old saves migrate** golf-surface areas into the grid on load.
