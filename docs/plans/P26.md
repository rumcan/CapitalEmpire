## 0. The plan on one screen
- **Three staff you hire, with monthly wages:** a teaching pro ($300) for each teaching hut, a head pro ($900) and a membership secretary
  ($450), who work from the pro's office. Hired and let go on a new Staff card.
- **Two new buildings:** the pro's office (art 53: $14,000, Course › Buildings, beside the clubhouse) and the teaching hut (art 54: $4,000,
  Facilities › Practice, at the range's mats or beside a practice green). Both are stand-ins until their art lands.
- **Members become a real number that the game keeps.** Today the game works them out: `monthlyMembershipIncome / MEMBER_DUES`. Under P26:
  - the player sets the dues a month, against a fair-price band;
  - members join from a pool (the town's golfers and the visitors who come back) and leave by churn, month by month;
  - the dues are booked monthly, not once a year at the tournament.
- **What the people do:**
  - the teaching pros give lessons at the range and the practice greens;
  - the head pro and the secretary sell memberships, keep members from leaving, and run the member events;
  - all of them walk the world, as stand-in people until art 136-138 is made.
- **The clubhouse's old "town visit" cars stay but become the bar and shop's trade:** $8 a visitor, not a $22 green fee. The halfway
  house takes its trade from T208's golfers instead of those cars.
- **The balance** is in section 6, modelled in range:gate's frame over 10 seasons:
  - a careful club with staff ends about $195,000 better off than under today's rules, with 135 members at $85 a month by season 10;
  - a careless club with no staff is about $26,000 worse off;
  - the staff are worth about $115,000 over the 10 seasons.

### 3.3 Selling memberships: the funnel from T208's visitors
1. **Visitors** come every town day (T208's tee sheet).
2. **The regulars** are the visitors who come back. Together with the town's own golfers they make up **the pool** (section 4.3): the
   golfers who might join.
3. **Each month a share of the pool joins.** The share is the base rate plus the staff's rates, times the club's appeal, times the price's
   effect (section 4.4).
4. **The sales show in the world:**
   - when a visitors' group checks in at the clubhouse, the head pro or the secretary walks out of the office and meets them at the door
     (a handshake: the idle clip facing them, until art 136's clip lands);
   - a green "+1 member" floats at the office;
   - with no staff, a sale is only the float, at the clubhouse's desk.
5. **How often:** a 10-minute town day is five game months, so a club selling 3 a month shows about 15 handshakes a day, about one every
   30 town minutes.
6. **Each month a share of the members leaves** (churn, section 4.5).
7. **The founding drive:** when the clubhouse opens, 40% of the pool joins in its first month, with a toast: "15 founding members".

## 4. Memberships as a real number
### 4.1 The price and the fair band
- **The player sets the dues a month** on the Clubhouse card: $5 steps, from $10 to $300. The start is $40.
- **The fair price:** F = (15 + 30 q + 15 r) x 1.4 on 18 holes x (1 + 0.08 x (clubhouse level − 1)). Here q is the quality and r the
  reputation, both 0..1, and the clubhouse level is 1-5.
- **The fair band is 0.85 F to 1.15 F.** It is drawn green under the price. Below the band the card says "Cheap: more join, the tee sheet
  fills". Above it the card says "Dear: fewer join, more leave".

| The club | q | r | Holes | Level | Fair price | Band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The first nine (season 2) | 0.85 | 0.56 | 9 | 1 | $49 | $42-56 |
| A good nine (season 5) | 0.85 | 0.73 | 9 | 2 | $56 | $47-64 |
| The first 18 (season 6) | 0.85 | 0.75 | 18 | 2 | $78 | $66-90 |
| A grown 18 (season 10) | 0.85 | 0.80 | 18 | 3 | $85 | $72-98 |
| Season 20 | 0.85 | 0.80 | 18 | 5 | $97 | $82-112 |

### 4.2 The money a member brings (a season, the game's 24 play days; P05 D6's 120 in brackets)
| Line | At $85 a month |
| --- | --- |
| Dues | +$1,020 |
| Lost green fees: each member is 0.15 visitors a day fewer (he was a regular), floored so the visitors never fall below half of `dailyGolfers`; 0.15 x 24 x $22 | −$79 (−$396) |

### 4.3 The pool: the town's size and the visitors who come back
- **The pool** = 4% of the residents + 0.8 x the visitors a day (`dailyGolfers`, the regulars from the town and the region). In 1985
  about 8% of Americans played golf, and about half of a town's golfers would think of joining.
- **It grows with T221's town:**

  | Season | Residents | Visitors a day | Pool |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 2 | 160 | 39 | 38 |
  | 5 | 823 | 48 | 71 |
  | 10 | 2,600 | 65 | 156 |
  | 20 | 5,541 | 65 | 274 |

### 4.4 Sales a month
- **Sales** = j x (pool − members), with j = (2% + 2.5% with a head pro + 2% with a secretary) x appeal x price effect. The 2% base means
  a club with no staff still sells (word of mouth at the clubhouse's desk).
- **The appeal** multiplies five terms:

  | Term | The formula | Its range |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | course quality | 0.5 + 0.5 q | 0.5 to 1 |
  | reputation | 0.7 + 0.6 r | 0.7 to 1.3 |
  | the clubhouse | 0.9 + 0.1 x (level − 1) | 0.9 to 1.3 |
  | the holes | 9 holes x 1, 18 holes x 1.15, fewer x holes / 9 | up to 1.15 |
  | the pace (T211) | 1 − 0.3 x slow; slow is 0 at a season's average wait of 5 minutes or less, 1 at 30 or more (`DaySeason`'s waits so far) | 0.7 to 1 |

- **The price effect** is clamp(1 + 2 x (1 − price / F), 0.1, 2):

  | Price | Price effect |
  | --- | --- |
  | 0.5 F | x 2 |
  | 0.85 F | x 1.3 |
  | F | x 1 |
  | 1.15 F | x 0.7 |
  | 1.5 F | x 0.1 |

- **The clubhouse level** (1-5, `LEVEL_XP` 400 / 1,200 / 3,000 / 7,000) grows from the clubhouse's visitors. From P26 it counts the bar's
  cars and every golfer who checks in, so it rises with use: level 2 in about season 5, level 3 in about season 9 for a careful club.
- **The country-club clubhouse** (art 167, "the club's top tier") is a later clubhouse upgrade. It is not in P26.

### 4.5 Churn a month
- **Churn** = 2% base, plus 4% x (price / F − 1) above the fair price, plus 1% x slow, minus the head pro's 0.8%, minus the secretary's 0.4%.
  It is never below 0.4%.
- **The members the pool settles at, at the fair price** (appeal about 0.88):

  | Staff | Joins a month | Churn a month | Members, as a share of the pool |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | none | 1.8% | 2.0% | 47% |
  | the head pro | 4.0% | 1.2% | 77% |
  | the head pro and the secretary | 5.7% | 0.8% | 88% |

- **The step is deterministic.** The members are kept as an expected count (a float in the save), and the cards show it rounded. The step
  runs once per game month at the month's start, in the same loop as the wages; a skip runs each month it crosses. So the numbers are
  testable and the same after a reload.

### 4.6 Members against green fees, and the tee sheet
- **Members take tee times.** On a day, `MEMBER_PLAY` (35%) of the members play, and the day's visitors are `max(0.5 D, D − 0.15 M)`
  (D: `dailyGolfers`, M: the members).
- **When the sheet is full, visitors are turned away** (T208's rule) and their fees are lost. A full sheet also slows the pace, which costs
  sales and members (4.4, 4.5). That is the cost of an underpriced club.
- **How full the sheet gets:**

  | Club | Sheet used |
  | --- | --- |
  | careful, season 10 | 45% |
  | careful, season 20 (250 members) | 59% |

- **A waiting list is a later idea:** the player closes the books at N members, and the fair price rises 10% while people wait. It is not in
  P26.

### 6.3 P05 (#413) and P26 together

### 6.4 The owner's questions (OWNER: balance calls; each has the lead's recommendation)
- **Q1 (OWNER): How big should memberships get?** As modelled, a careful club's dues pass its green fees from the first 18 holes and reach
  about $130,000 a season by season 10. That is about four times today's memberships, and about a quarter of the careful club's income in
  the campaign's frame. *Recommended: as modelled.* If you want them smaller, halve the town's share of the pool (4% → 2%, `p26-members
  --pool-town 0.02`): 91 members and $89,000 of dues at season 10; the careful club's 10-season gain is then +$68,000 and the careless
  club's loss −$54,000.
- **Q2 (OWNER): Is the early dip all right?** In seasons 2-5 a club earns $3,000-12,000 a season less from memberships than today, because
  nobody hands them out for free any more. *Recommended: accept it.* The founding drive (40% of the pool) and the 2% base sales already
  soften it, and P05 D6 sets the tier-1 money anyway.
- **Q3 (OWNER): Are the wages right?** Head pro $900, teaching pro $300, secretary $450 a month. *Recommended: as they are.* Each pays back
  within one to two seasons once the club is big enough for it (6.2).
- **Q4 (OWNER): The clubhouse's old visit cars.** Make them the bar and the shop's trade at $8 a visitor (+20% with a head pro), or drop
  them. *Recommended: $8.* They keep the car park and the clubhouse's level alive; the line falls from about $9,300 to $3,400 a season at
  2,600 residents (more as the clubhouse levels up).
- **Q5 (OWNER): If P05 D6 (120 play days) is approved, should the fair price rise with it?** The lost fees a member costs grow fivefold,
  which takes the careful club's gain from +$195,000 to +$77,000. *Recommended: no; re-run `p26-members --days 120` after P05 and decide
  then.*

## 8. Save compatibility
- **Members were never saved; they were worked out.** The yearly number was the tournament close's `annualMembershipIncome`.
- **A save without `golf.club`** (every save before P26) loads like this:

  | What | On load |
  | --- | --- |
  | Members | `legacyMonthlyMembership(q, rep, holes) / 50`: exactly the count its Clubhouse card showed. q, rep and holes come from `golf.everyday.inp` when present, else from the course stats at the first frame. |
  | Price | $50: what the old dues were |
  | The founding drive | marked done, so an old club gets no windfall |
  | Staff | none |

  The members then follow P26's monthly step.
- **The season of the load** books part of its memberships the old way. The months of the season that had passed before the load are
  booked at that season's tournament close, at the old annual rate times (months before the load) / 12. This is saved as
  `golf.club.legacyMonths` and dropped after that close. The months after the load are booked monthly. So a season that straddles the
  load is booked once, neither twice nor half.
- **A founding season** (no tournament) booked no memberships under the old rule. It now gets the months after the load.
- **A mid-day reload:**
  - T208's `DaySave.inp` without `members` rebuilds that day's roster with today's formula, so the same golfers come back;
  - the next day's inputs carry the members.
- **`YearEndResult`** keeps `membershipIncome` (now the season's dues as booked) and `monthlyMembership` (now this month's dues). An old
  results screen reads as before.
- **The new buildings** are businesses (P26.D8). An old save has none; the business list's normalizer is unchanged.
- **Tests:**
  - `golf-save-compat` loads the range20 fixture and an old-start (`?start=town`) save. Each must keep the same member count on the card at
    load, book the season's memberships within $1 of the old rule's total for that season, and then step on.
  - `save-fixtures` passes unchanged.

## 9. Risks
- **The pool's town share (4%)** sets the size of the whole feature (Q1). It is one constant, and the model can be re-run in a second.
- **The range hut and the academy share one demand.** A player with the academy who builds a range hut gains only the overflow. The hut's
  Build row says "With the academy: only when its bays are busy".
- **A full tee sheet at season 20** (59%) is where T211's waits start to bite. That is wanted (the cost of cheap dues), but the pace term
  needs watching once the sheet passes 70%.
- **The handshakes** are drawn at the clubhouse door, where the cars of T208's arrivals and the tournament crowd's doors also are. They are
  off on a tournament day (the course is closed then anyway).
