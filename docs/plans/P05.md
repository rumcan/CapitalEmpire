# P05: M5 Balance: economy, upkeep, crowd per tier (Alpha 1.0)

Status: **draft for the owner's approval** (2026-10-27; risks R1 and R2 measured the same day, sections 1h and 1i). Issue #68. No game numbers change until it is approved.

Scope: tiers 1-3 are balanced (Alpha 1.0, plans/P01.md); tiers 4-5 are only measured here (they are smoke-tested in Alpha).

## Summary
**Today:**
- The simulator (`golf/campaign.ts`) and the game disagree badly, because the simulator flatters the game.
  - Its tents reach the whole crowd; the game's reach a third, or a sixth on 18 holes.
  - Its second nine costs $60,000. In the game nine more holes fit on the starting land on every seed tested, for about $193,000 at seed (1i).
  - It has no car parks to buy. The game's tier-3 crowd needs about 50 of them, about $400,000 (1h).
  - A careful player in the game's own numbers reaches tier 3 in year 4 but runs about $100,000 into debt that year. The simulator says tier 3 in year 5 with $366,000 to spare.
- **The car park caps the crowd** (measured in the game, 1h). The start's car park holds 56 cars, which is 168 fans:
  - a first tier-1 tournament gets 47% of its crowd in, and a tier-2 one 10%;
  - with car parks built, 100% get in at tier 1 and 70-81% at tier 2 (the rest are held in junction jams).
- **Course upkeep** is charged at a hidden 25%. The full rate double-counts a 54-ha base rough.
- **The club's year** (green fees, memberships) counts only 24 play days.
- **Course quality** does not reach the crowd.

**The proposal:**
- The game gets a crowd hub and realistic upkeep and club income.
- The simulator runs on the game's own functions.
- The start's car park stays as it is, and the player is shown the busiest day's cars against the bays (D13).

With the proposed numbers (modelled):
- a tent pays back in 1.8-2.6 tournaments at the tier it is meant for (the merch tent 2.5 at tier 3);
- tier 1 bites (−$17k in year 1) but never bankrupts;
- tier 3 comes in year 4: the second nine and the tier-3 car parks dip the treasury to about $62k, then tier 3 nets $160k-190k a year;
- each tier's crowd is a clear step up;
- course quality moves the crowd by up to 15%.

## How it was measured
Headless, with the game's own code; scripts in the lead's scratch folder, nothing committed.
- **The real course:** `planGolfGame(generateTerrain(seed))`, the old nine of a new game, seeds 1, 3, 4.
- **Its holes:** `deriveHoles`.
- **Its upkeep and build cost:** `costs.ts`.
- **Its quality:** `buildCourseStatsPanel`.
- **Tents:** `evaluateEventToolDay`, through `computeYearEnd`'s own path (multi-day and practice days included).
- **The simulator:** `simulateCampaign`.
- **18 holes** for the tent reach: the real nine plus a second generated nine. The real nine's last green stays the 18th green.

- **The arrival rate** (the share of the demand that gets in): the real game in headless Edge, on tiers 1 and 2 (1h). The simulator assumes 60% for a careful player. Sections 1c-1g were measured at an assumed 60% (and 43%) before that.
- **Whether a second nine fits on the starting land:** the game's own start and New hole geometry, headless (1i).

## 3. The balance tests (`tests/golf-balance-p05.test.ts`)
1. **Payback at the tier a structure is meant for:** the food tent, drinks tent and kiosk at tier 2, and the merch tent at tier 3, pay back in 2-4 tournaments. Measured at the hub, at the measured arrival (0.75 at tier 2, 0.6 at tier 3), entry reputation, on the canonical course.
2. **Upkeep bites, never bankrupts:**
   - a careful tier-1 year on the canonical nine at quality 85 nets between −$25,000 and +$10,000;
   - a quality-50 course nets less;
   - the careless campaign never goes below $400,000 in 8 years and never passes tier 1.
3. **Crowd steps:** spectator-days rise at least ×2 per tier; quality 100 against 50 moves the demand by 15% (±1%).
4. **The simulator agrees with the game:** for every year of the careful campaign, `simulateCampaign`'s tickets, tents, club income and course upkeep are within 10% of `computeYearEnd` + `computeTournamentCloseBook` on the same canonical course at the same arrival.
5. **The campaign's shape:**
   - the careful campaign reaches tier 3 by year 5 at the measured arrival (1.0 / 0.75 / 0.6), with car parks bought and a min treasury of at least $25,000;
   - a careful tier-3 year nets at least $100,000 more than the careless player's best year.

## 4. Open risks
- **R1, tier 3's arrival and parking.** Tiers 1-2 are measured (1h): 100% and about 75% with car parks built, and the start's car park alone caps any crowd at 168 fans. Tier 3 is open:
  - its final day sends about 2,300 cars, which needs about 50 car parks ($400k);
  - the tier-2 crowd already loses 20-30% in junction jams.

  The model uses 60%. At 45%, the careful player's lowest treasury falls from $62k to $17k. T-D measures tier 3: car parks built, whether 50 fit near the venue, and the park-and-ride's share.
- **R2, the second nine's price: measured, and settled for tier 3** (1i).
  - Nine more holes fit on the starting land on every seed tested: about $193k at seed, about $305k with greens and tees sodded.
  - Land is needed only for tier 4's 6,400 m.
  - What remains: the 6-month grow-in of seeded greens. A player who sods pays $114k more and must wait a year.
- **R3, the hub's weight** (D1: the greens' total) is a judgment. It makes placement matter (hub against course: 66% against 6-11%) without making every tent a goldmine. The exam is T-A's screenshot of a tournament with tents at both.
- **R4, the careless player out-saves the careful one until year 9.** At the measured rates the careful player invests about $731k. Its year-8 treasury ($769k) is just under the careless player's ($788k), though it nets $188k a year against $45k.

  The careless player's money comes from its club, whose income rises with reputation, and its reputation climbs at tier 1 without any investment (44 → 74). With a full tier-1 crowd it would reach 93 and $930k (D13).

  Knobs, modelled over 12 years. These are proposals only; the game is unchanged.

  | knob | careful: year-8 treasury | careless: year-8 treasury | careful ahead from | careful: min treasury |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | none (the proposal) | $769,036 | $788,000 | year 9 | $61,854 |
  | sponsors × (1 + (rep − 50) / 50), both players (+60% at rep 80) | $878,288 | $788,000 | **year 8** | $82,596 |
  | the club's year × (1 + 0.15 × (tier − 1)): prestige, as a club hosting bigger events draws more members and rounds | $1,069,044 | $788,000 | year 7 | $138,576 |
  | the same at 0.25 | $1,269,049 | $788,000 | year 6 | $189,723 |
  | course upkeep × quality / 85 | $769,036 | $870,447 | year 9 | $61,854 |

  - **The sponsor bonus is the gentlest.** It pays only from tier 2, so the careless player is untouched, and it moves the crossover to year 8 (+$24k a year at tier 3).
  - **Club prestige is stronger and fits the fiction.** But it adds $57-95k a year at tier 3, and tier 3's targets would need checking again.
  - **Upkeep tied to quality runs the wrong way.** The careless course (quality 75) gets cheaper to keep, so the careless player saves more.
  - **Recommendation:** the sponsor bonus at k = 1, or no knob if a year-9 crossover is acceptable.
- **R5, tiers 4-5:** chalets and the pavilion pay back in under one tournament, and tier 5 nets about $6M a year. Out of Alpha scope; P05b balances them with staff costs (D11) and TV.
- **R6, saved games:** D3-D6 change a loaded game's money from the next booking. Accepted for Alpha (the testers start new games).
