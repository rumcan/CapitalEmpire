# P05: M5 Balance: economy, upkeep, crowd per tier (Alpha 1.0)

Status: **draft for the owner's approval** (2026-10-27). Issue #68. No game numbers change until it is approved.

Scope: tiers 1-3 are balanced (Alpha 1.0, plans/P01.md); tiers 4-5 are only measured here (they are smoke-tested in Alpha).

## Summary
**Today:**
- The simulator (`golf/campaign.ts`) and the game disagree badly, because the simulator flatters the game.
  - Its tents reach the whole crowd; the game's reach a third, or a sixth on 18 holes.
  - Its second nine costs $60,000; the game's costs about $505,000.
  - A careful player in the game's own numbers stalls at tier 2 for good. The simulator says it reaches tier 3 in year 5.
- **Course upkeep** is charged at a hidden 25%. The full rate double-counts a 54-ha base rough.
- **The club's year** (green fees, memberships) counts only 24 play days.
- **Course quality** does not reach the crowd.

**The proposal:**
- The game gets a crowd hub and realistic upkeep and club income.
- The simulator runs on the game's own functions.

With the proposed numbers (modelled):
- a tent pays back in 2.4-3.5 tournaments at the tier it is meant for;
- tier 1 bites (−$17k in year 1) but never bankrupts;
- the second nine is a real milestone (the treasury dips to about $45k, then tier 3 earns $160k+ a year);
- each tier's crowd is a clear step up;
- course quality moves the crowd by up to 15%.

## How it was measured
Headless, with the game's own code; scripts in the lead's scratch folder, nothing committed.
- **The real course:** `planGolfGame(generateTerrain(seed))`, the old nine of a new game, seeds 1, 3, 4.
- **Its holes:** `deriveHoles`.
- **Its upkeep and build cost:** `costs.ts`.
- **Its quality:** `buildCourseStatsPanel`.
- **Tents:** `evaluateEventToolDay`, through `computeYearEnd`'s own path (multi-day and practice days included).
- **The simulator:** `simulateCampaign`.
- **18 holes** for the tent reach: the real nine plus a second generated nine. The real nine's last green stays the 18th green.

The game's arrival rate (the share of the demand that gets in) is not measured here; that needs a browser run. Its last browser measurement, the first tournament on 2026-10-22, was 43%. The simulator assumes 60% for a careful player.

## 2. Decisions (proposed; the owner approves)
The targets:
- a structure pays back in about 2-4 tournaments at the tier it is meant for (the tier whose needs list it, or the first tier where it pays);
- upkeep bites but never bankrupts a sane course;
- each tier's crowd is a clear step up;
- the simulator and the game agree within about 10% at the same arrival.

## 3. The balance tests (`tests/golf-balance-p05.test.ts`)
1. **Payback at the tier a structure is meant for:** the food tent, drinks tent and kiosk at tier 2, and the merch tent at tier 3, pay back in 2-4 tournaments. Measured at the hub, arrival 0.6, entry reputation, on the canonical course.
2. **Upkeep bites, never bankrupts:**
   - a careful tier-1 year on the canonical nine at quality 85 nets between −$25,000 and +$10,000;
   - a quality-50 course nets less;
   - the careless campaign never goes below $400,000 in 8 years and never passes tier 1.
3. **Crowd steps:** spectator-days rise at least ×2 per tier; quality 100 against 50 moves the demand by 15% (±1%).
4. **The simulator agrees with the game:** for every year of the careful campaign, `simulateCampaign`'s tickets, tents, club income and course upkeep are within 10% of `computeYearEnd` + `computeTournamentCloseBook` on the same canonical course at the same arrival.
5. **The campaign's shape:** the careful campaign reaches tier 3 by year 7 at arrival 0.6 with a min treasury of at least $25,000; a careful tier-3 year nets at least $100,000 more than the careless player's best year.
6. **Updated existing tests:** `golf-campaign.test.ts` "year-by-year progression" (tier 3 moves from year 5 to year 6) and "capital investments" (the expansion's price); `golf-balance-income.test.ts` (play days, upkeep share); `golf-yearend.test.ts` and `golf-tentmoney.test.ts` (the hub).

## 4. Open risks
- **R1, the arrival rate:** everything scales with it, and the game's last measurement (43%, 2026-10-22) predates many movers changes. T-D measures it per tier. If it is far below 43%, D6 or the tickets need another pass.
- **R2, the second nine's price:** it depends on what the player buys and paints. A player who fits 18 holes of 5,800 m on the starting 54-ha parcel pays no land (about $290k), and tier 3 comes a year earlier. T-D checks whether that fits.
- **R3, the hub's weight** (D1: the greens' total) is a judgment. It makes placement matter (hub against course: 66% against 6-11%) without making every tent a goldmine. The exam is T-A's screenshot of a tournament with tents at both.
- **R4, the careless player out-saves the careful one for about 9 years:** the careful player invests $651k. If the owner wants the careful player ahead by year 8, raise the tier-3 sponsor or ticket, or lower the second nine's land price.
- **R5, tiers 4-5:** chalets and the pavilion pay back in under one tournament, and tier 5 nets about $6M a year. Out of Alpha scope; P05b balances them with staff costs (D11) and TV.
- **R6, saved games:** D3-D6 change a loaded game's money from the next booking. Accepted for Alpha (the testers start new games).
