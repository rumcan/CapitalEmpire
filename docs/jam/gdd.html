Fairway Fortune

Build a golf club from a driving range. Every swing of the business is a money decision.
Genre: management simulation with arcade golf
Platform: web browser (desktop) for the jam; PC (Steam) and tablets next
Audience: players 16+ who like tycoon, builder and sports games; schools and financial-education programmes
Session: 20 to 40 minutes for the jam challenge; open-ended after it
Jam: Zoud GameLab, theme "Make FinLit Fun"
Version: jam build, November 2026

1. Game overview

You own a plot of land on a country road, a little cash and a driving range. Golfers drive in, park, buy buckets and hit balls. That small, steady income is your first business. You use it to build a golf course hole by hole: shape fairways and greens, place bunkers, ponds and trees, lay cart paths, open a clubhouse. Then you walk out and play the holes you built in a painted, behind-the-golfer golf view.

Money is the language of every choice. A new hole costs more than the range earns in a month: do you save up, borrow from the bank at compound interest, or use Islamic financing with a fixed, agreed markup? A storm floods your bunkers: does your emergency fund or your insurance cover it, or do you slide into an expensive overdraft? Raise the bucket price and you earn more per golfer but see fewer of them. Each month ends with a statement: what came in, what went out, what the interest cost you, and your net worth.

The jam challenge: turn the range into a nine-hole golf club within two seasons, finishing with a net worth of at least $250,000 and no overdraft. A report card grades how you did it: debt left, interest paid against interest earned, emergencies weathered, holes built.

2. Core gameplay

Run the range→Earn and save→Choose how to pay→Build holes and facilities→Golfers pay green fees→Play your holes→Month-end statement→Emergencies and choices↺

Build

  • Driving range: bays, targets, a ball shop, coffee, lessons; upgrades such as covered bays, floodlights and a pro shop. Set the bucket price.
  • Course: place holes from tees to greens; paint fairway, rough, bunkers and water; plant trees; shape the ground; lay cart paths and a car park.
  • Club: a clubhouse with green fees, staff with wages, practice areas.

Play

  • "Play this hole" opens a painted, behind-the-golfer golf view of the exact hole you built: its trees, slopes, rough and hazards.
  • Hold to swing and release at the right strength; click the ground to aim; a full 14-club bag; the lie (rough, sand) costs distance.
  • Playing your own course is how you feel its quality: a hole that is no fun to play will not keep paying golfers.

Manage money

  • The Bank: loans with a chosen amount and term (APR, monthly payment and total interest shown before signing); murabaha financing for a named purchase; a savings account; your credit score.
  • Protection: no insurance, basic or full cover, each with a monthly premium and a deductible.
  • Month end: instalments are paid, savings earn interest, an overdraft costs interest, and a statement toast shows profit and the change in net worth.

Controls

Mouse throughout: the tool rail on the left, cards on the right, click and drag to build. In the golf view: hold the swing button (or Space) and release; click the ground to aim; number keys or the wheel for clubs.

3. Financial literacy integration

Fairway Fortune does not explain finance with text screens. Each concept is a system the player runs into and has to handle, and the consequences show up in the month's statement.

MechanicConceptWhat the player experiences and learns
Bucket price, green feePricing, demandA higher price earns more per golfer but brings fewer; the cards show expected golfers and takings at nearby prices.
Bank loanDebt, APR, compound interestBorrowing builds a hole now; the total interest is shown before signing and is paid every month after. A longer term means smaller payments but more interest.
MurabahaAlternative (Islamic) financingThe bank buys the item and sells it to you at cost plus a markup fixed up front. The price never grows; paying late costs a fixed charity donation, never interest. Shown side by side with a loan.
Savings accountSaving, interest earnedCash put aside earns interest monthly and becomes the emergency fund.
Random emergenciesEmergency fund, riskStorms, a broken pump, a drought bill. With a fund they are an inconvenience; without one they push you into overdraft.
Insurance plansPremium, deductible, risk transferPay a small amount every month to cap a large unknown cost; see what the insurer paid and what you paid.
OverdraftHigh-cost debtRunning out of cash is allowed but costs about 34% a year, and lasting overdraft ends the game.
Credit scoreCreditworthinessPaying on time lowers the rate offered on the next loan; missed payments raise it.
Upgrades and buildingsInvestment, payback, opportunity costEach purchase shows its cost, upkeep, expected extra income and the months until it pays back.
Monthly statement, net worthCash flow, profit, assets and liabilitiesProfit is not cash and cash is not wealth: the statement and the net-worth chart show all three.
Report cardReflection, financial goalsThe two-season goal is graded with reasons, inviting another run with a different strategy.

Learning outcomes

  • Compare the total cost of credit options before borrowing (APR, term, total interest; interest-based and markup-based financing).
  • Explain why an emergency fund and insurance protect a plan, and how a premium and a deductible work.
  • Read a simple income statement and tell profit, cash flow and net worth apart.
  • Judge an investment by its payback time and its running costs.
  • See how prices trade volume for margin.

Behavioural design

The game puts known biases on the table instead of lecturing about them. Present bias: borrowing to build now is tempting, and the statement shows the interest month after month. Optimism bias: emergencies are rare, so skipping insurance feels free until one hits. Mental accounting: the range has its own till and the club its treasury, so where money sits matters. Short one-line "money moments" appear the first time each consequence happens (your first month of interest, your first emergency without a fund), so the lesson arrives at the moment of the experience.

Accuracy

Loan payments use the standard amortisation formula with monthly compounding. Murabaha follows the common cost-plus-markup instalment sale. The AED display uses the fixed peg of 3.6725 AED per US dollar. Numbers are simplified for play and stated as such.

4. Visual style and player experience

  • World: a hand-painted isometric countryside with a single sun at the upper right, a living range with golfers who drive in, park and hit balls, and grass, sand and water textures with no visible tiling.
  • Golf view: a painted pixel look with an anti-flicker filter and a light cinematic finish at golden hour, using the same golfer art as the builder.
  • First minutes: a guided start (place the range, the car park, sell your first buckets, build your first hole) with tools unlocking as you grow.
  • Accessibility and inclusion: adjustable interface size; gains and losses marked by sign and arrow, not only by colour; plain-language explanations on every financial term (hover, tap or keyboard focus); US dollars or UAE dirhams.
  • Regional relevance: murabaha financing alongside conventional loans, AED display, and desert courses among the biomes.

5. Development and future plans

Built during the jam

The course-building engine and the golf view existed as a work in progress before the jam. During the jam it was rebuilt around financial literacy: the Bank (loans, murabaha, savings, overdraft, credit score), monthly accounting and statements, emergencies and insurance, payback and price readouts, the glossary, the two-season challenge and report card, and a focused jam build with unfinished systems removed.

Roadmap after the jam

  • Season mode: tournaments with sponsors, prize purses and TV deals; a career across several clubs.
  • Deeper finance: a share issue to bring in investors (equity versus debt), leasing versus buying equipment, taxes, inflation.
  • Arabic localisation with right-to-left layout and a review of the financial content by an Islamic finance advisor.
  • Classroom mode: fixed scenarios, a teacher dashboard and discussion prompts after each report card.
  • Platforms: Steam (PC), then tablets.

The production plan and costs are in the separate budget document.
