Fairway Fortune

Build a golf club from a driving range, manage its money, and play the holes you built.
Genre: management simulation with arcade golf
Platform: web browser (desktop) for the jam; PC (Steam) and tablets next
Audience: players 16+ who like tycoon, builder and sports games; schools and financial-education programmes
Session: 20 to 40 minutes to grow the first holes; open-ended after that
Jam: Zoud GameLab, theme "Make FinLit Fun"
Build: the jam MVP, November 2026

1. Game overview

You have $75,000, and by a country road stands an old driving range for sale: a dirt car park, four mats, a ball picker, a part-time attendant and an outfield full of junk. Buy it, and golfers start to drive in, buy buckets and hit balls. That small, steady income is your business. Run it well (set the bucket price, clear the junk, upgrade the bays) and it pays for a golf course, one hole at a time, and a clubhouse that charges green fees. Nothing is forced: a Build menu with pictures shows every option, its price and its payback, in any order.

Then you walk out and play the holes you built in a painted, behind-the-golfer golf view. Between rounds you practise on your own range and chipping green: drills unlock shot shaping, flight control and the putt read, and lower your handicap.

Every step is a money decision you make yourself: what to charge, what to build next, whether an upgrade will pay for itself, and whether to wait and save or borrow now and pay interest. Each month ends with a short statement of what came in, what went out and what you are worth.

2. Core gameplay

Run the range→Set prices, fetch balls→Save or borrow→Build holes and a clubhouse→Green fees→Play your holes→Practise, earn skills→Month-end statement↺

Run the business

  • Driving range: buy the old range (it comes with a dirt car park, a picker and an attendant whose wage is a monthly cost), clear the junk for a little scrap, set the bucket price, buy range upgrades.
  • Course: the New hole tool lays a hole from tee to green on your land; holes open once the range has proved itself.
  • Clubhouse: golfers pay green fees for every round; more holes, more fees.
  • The Bank: opened when you choose: borrow (the monthly payment, the rate and the total interest are shown before you sign), repay early, or move cash into savings that earn interest.

Play and practise

  • "Play this hole" opens the exact hole you built, with its trees, slopes and hazards. Hold to swing and release at the right strength; click the ground to aim; a 14-club bag; rough and sand cost distance, so you club up.
  • "Hit balls" and "Chip and putt" put your golfer on a range mat or at the chipping green. Six drills (distance control, shot shaping, flight, up and down, putting, bunker) each grant skill once: shaping, flight and the putt read then work on the course, and the handicap drops.

Controls

Mouse throughout: the tool rail on the left, one card at a time on the right, click to build. In the golf view: hold the swing button (or Space) and release; click the ground to aim; number keys or the wheel for clubs.

3. Financial literacy integration

The game teaches through the money the player manages, not through lessons. There are no quizzes or text screens: the consequences arrive in the cash figure and in the month's statement.

Learning outcomes

  • Price for a profit, knowing that higher prices bring fewer customers.
  • Judge an investment by its payback time and its running costs.
  • Compare saving up with borrowing, and read a loan's rate, term and total interest before signing.
  • Tell profit, cash flow and net worth apart on a simple monthly statement.

Accuracy

Loan payments use the standard amortisation formula with monthly compounding; savings interest is credited monthly. The AED display uses the fixed peg of 3.6725 AED per US dollar. Numbers are simplified for play.

4. Visual style and player experience

  • World: a hand-painted isometric countryside in autumn or desert, with a single sun at the upper right and a living range: golfers drive in, park, queue at the shop and hit balls that land on the target greens.
  • Golf view: a painted pixel look with an anti-flicker filter, using the same golfer art as the builder and the same interface style.
  • First minutes: a title card, a short three-step welcome and optional tips; the player decides what to build and when.
  • Accessibility: adjustable text size; gains and losses marked by sign and arrow, not only by colour; US dollars or UAE dirhams; mouse-only play.

5. Development and future plans

The jam MVP

The course-building engine and the golf view existed as a work in progress before the jam. During the jam they were cut down to a tested core loop and rebuilt around the money the player manages: the Bank and monthly accounts, payback and price readouts, the month-end statement, the practice mode with its skill drills, a new title card and tutorial, and a single interface style across the builder and the golf view. Systems that were built but not yet tested enough were hidden from the jam build; they are the roadmap below.
