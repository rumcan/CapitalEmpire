Golf Manager 2026

Pick your spot, open a driving range, build a golf course hole by hole, then walk out and play it from the tee.
Genre: golf course builder and tycoon, with arcade golf you play yourself
Platform: web browser (desktop) for the jam; PC (Steam) and tablets next
Audience: players 16+ who like theme-park, tycoon, builder and sports games; schools and financial-education programmes
Session: 20 to 40 minutes to grow the first holes; open-ended after that
Jam: Zoud GameLab, theme "Make FinLit Fun"
Build: the jam MVP, November 2026

1. Game overview

Golf Manager 2026 is a golf club you build and then play. A new game opens on an empty valley: you place your driving range where you like (or let the game pick a spot), and that one click buys the land, lays the county road past it, sets down your home and opens an old range with a dirt car park, four mats and an outfield full of junk. Golfers start to drive in, park, buy a bucket and hit balls at the target greens, and a small town across the road grows as your club does.

The range is your first business. Clear the junk, upgrade the bays and the ball room, paint and prop the outfield, set the bucket price. When it has earned its keep the course opens: the New hole tool lays a hole from a tee and a green you click on your land, with natural contours on its green, and a big BUILD NOW button builds it. Then make it yours: paint greens, fairways, bunkers and water by hand, paint the ground with the Texture Painter, plant trees and flower beds and place course props. Or import a real golf course and play it. Add a clubhouse, and every round pays green fees.

Then the best part: play the holes you built, from the tee, in a painted behind-the-golfer golf view. Hold to swing and tap to boost the power; shape the shot with draw, fade and flight; spin the ball in the air with the arrow keys; read the green on a slope grid coloured like a heat map; play in daylight, golden hour, dusk or at night. Between rounds, practise on your own range and chipping green: a launch-monitor readout after every ball and six drills that unlock skills and lower your handicap.

Every build is paid from your own takings, a bank loan or your savings. The money is always on screen, but it never gets in the way: what to build next, whether to wait or borrow, and what each thing earns are the player's own calls.

2. Core gameplay

Place your range→Run and grow it→Build holes and a clubhouse→Play your holes from the tee→Practise, earn skills→Green fees, the month's statement↺

Build

  • Choose where to start: the driving range is in hand, green where it fits and red with the reason where it does not; R turns it, and "Pick a spot for me" places it for you. The click buys the land, the home, the road and the old range ($15,000 of the $75,000 start).
  • Driving range: clear the junk for a little scrap, set the bucket price, buy upgrades in the ball room tree (pickers, staff, supply), paint the outfield, place props and flags. Golfers and staff walk your paths.
  • Build menu: picture cards for everything you can build, each with its price, its monthly upkeep and when it pays for itself; build in any order.
  • Course: New hole lays a hole from a tee and a green you click (BUILD NOW on the map builds it); its green gets natural contours. Holes and the clubhouse open after about two months of range takings.
  • Shape it by hand: paint greens, fairways, bunkers and water with brushes; the Texture Painter paints the ground's look; Plants (trees and flower beds) and Props (course furniture and decoration) dress the course.
  • Real courses: import a real golf course (from OpenStreetMap data) and play it in the golf view.
  • The Bank: click your cash: borrow (the payment, the rate and the total interest shown before you sign), repay early, or save.

Play

  • Play this hole opens the exact hole you built, with its trees, slopes, sand and water, in the painted golf view. A hole minimap at the top right, a collapsed HUD that keeps the golfer in view, and a shot panel that folds away at the side.
  • The swing: hold to swing and release; tap Up while holding to boost (a little more power, more with the Go for it charge); click the ground to aim; 14 clubs; rough and sand cost distance. While the ball flies, the arrows add backspin, topspin or side spin.
  • Shot shaping: move the dot on the ball for a draw or a fade and choose a high or low flight; what a golfer can do grows with his handicap.
  • Putting: a grid over the green, each line green on the flat through yellow to red where it is steep, with dots that run downhill along it.
  • Hazards: the cliffs and rock beside the holes are a lateral hazard: a penalty stroke and a drop.
  • Time of day: day, golden hour, dusk or night, picked on the hole card.
  • Practice: "Hit balls" and "Chip and putt" put your golfer on a range mat or the chipping green. After each ball a readout shows carry, total, ball and club speed, spin and a tip. Six drills (distance, shaping, flight, up and down, putting, bunker) each grant a skill once.

Controls

Builder: the mouse throughout (the tool rail at the left, one card at a time at the right, click to build; R turns what you place; the wheel zooms, a drag pans). Golf view: hold the swing button or Space and let go; tap Up while holding to boost; the arrows spin the ball in flight; click the ground to aim; 1 to 9 or the wheel for clubs; Esc back to the course. Settings: Exit to main menu (it saves), text size, US dollars or UAE dirhams, the radio's music.

3. Financial literacy integration

The game teaches through the money the player manages, not through lessons. There are no quizzes or text screens: the consequences arrive in the cash figure and in the month's statement.

What the player doesConceptWhat they experience
Sets the bucket pricePricing and demandA higher price earns more per golfer but brings fewer; the card shows the golfers and takings expected at nearby prices.
Keeps or lets go the attendantRunning costs, wagesThe attendant's wage comes out every month and keeps the range in balls; picking them up yourself saves the wage but costs your time.
Buys an upgrade or a buildingInvestment, paybackEach purchase shows its cost, its monthly upkeep, the extra income expected and "pays back in about N months", or that it never will.
Builds the next hole now or laterSaving versus borrowingWaiting costs nothing but time; a loan builds it today. The Bank shows the total interest before signing, and the payment comes out every month after.
Chooses the loan termInterest and termA longer term means smaller payments but more interest in total; repaying early saves interest.
Keeps cash in savingsSaving, interest earnedSavings earn a little every month; cash in hand earns nothing.
Reads the month-end statementCash flow, profit, net worthProfit is not cash and cash is not wealth: the statement and the net-worth line show all three.
Practises instead of buildingInvesting in skillsTime on the range does not earn money, but the skills it unlocks make every later round better.

Learning outcomes

  • Price for a profit, knowing that higher prices bring fewer customers.
  • Judge an investment by its payback time and its running costs.
  • Compare saving up with borrowing, and read a loan's rate, term and total interest before signing.
  • Tell profit, cash flow and net worth apart on a simple monthly statement.

Accuracy

Loan payments use the standard amortisation formula with monthly compounding; savings interest is credited monthly. The AED display uses the fixed peg of 3.6725 AED per US dollar. Numbers are simplified for play.

4. Visual style and player experience

  • World: a hand-painted isometric countryside, with a single sun at the upper right and a living club: golfers drive in, park, walk the paths, queue at the shop and hit balls that land on the target greens; farm animals in the fields and a town across the road.
  • Golf view: a painted pixel look behind the golfer, the course you built with its contours, cliffs and rocks, four lights from day to night, and the same golfer art and interface style as the builder.
  • Sound: every effect synthesized in code (the swish and strike of each club, the landings, the cup, the crowd) and a radio in the top bar with free CC0 music.
  • First minutes: a title card over a living scene of a club (New game, Continue), who-are-you, then placing the driving range; a short three-step tutorial with a "Show me" on each step, and optional tips; the player decides what to build and when.
  • Accessibility: adjustable text size; gains and losses marked by sign and arrow, not only by colour; US dollars or UAE dirhams; mouse-only play.

5. Development and future plans

The jam MVP

The course-building engine and the golf view existed as a work in progress before the jam. During the jam they were cut down to a tested core loop: choosing where to start, the range and its upgrades, New hole with BUILD NOW, painting the course by hand, the Texture Painter, Plants and Props, real-course import, the golf view's new interface (boost, air spin, shaping, the putting grid, the time of day, cliffs as a lateral hazard), the practice mode with its readout and drills, the Bank and the month's statement, a title card over a living scene, the radio, and a painted picture on every action. Systems that were built but not yet tested enough were hidden from the jam build; they are the roadmap below.
