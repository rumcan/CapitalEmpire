Fairway Fortune: Budget and Production Plan

From the jam build to a 1.0 release on PC, with a classroom edition. All amounts in US dollars.
Summary. A 12-month plan in three milestones that takes the jam build to an Early Access release on Steam and a classroom edition for schools and financial-education programmes. The full plan costs $68,750. A reduced plan for $30,000 is shown in section 4. The engine, art pipeline and golf view already exist, so the money goes into content, localisation, expert review, polish and launch rather than technology.

1. Starting point

  • A working course-building engine in the browser (WebGL2, TypeScript), a living driving range, and a painted golf view that plays the holes the player built.
  • The jam MVP's money layer and practice mode: the Bank (loans, savings), monthly statements, payback and price readouts, and skill drills on the range. Emergencies and insurance, murabaha financing and a graded challenge are built and scheduled for testing in M1.
  • An AI-assisted production pipeline for code and 2D art, directed and reviewed by the developer, which keeps the team small and the costs low.

5. Market and revenue

  • Players: management and sports-sim players on PC; golf and tycoon games sell steadily, and a game that plays the course you built is a clear hook.
  • Education: schools and financial-education programmes, including in the UAE and the wider region, where Islamic finance is part of everyday banking.
  • Model: premium game at $12.99 on Steam with no microtransactions and no loot boxes, in keeping with its message; a classroom licence at $99 per school per year; white-label scenario packs for banks and financial-literacy programmes.
